Resume Architect Privacy Notice

Effective date: November 2, 2026

This notice applies to Resume Architect and related career-document tools, including integrations used to retrieve public job information or authorized account information for resume, career, and profile support.

Information the application may process

The application may process information supplied by the user, including resumes, career history, education, training, qualifications, job-targeting preferences, professional profile text, and other information the user chooses to provide.

When connected services are enabled, the application may also process information returned by those services within the permissions granted to the application.

USAJOBS integration

The application may retrieve public job-announcement information from USAJOBS through its official developer interfaces. This may include job titles, announcement numbers, agencies, occupational series, grade information, locations, duties, qualifications, specialized-experience requirements, hiring paths, open and close dates, required documents, and related public vacancy information.

The application is designed not to send unnecessary resume information or personal identifiers to USAJOBS when performing vacancy searches.

Professional-network integration

Where an authorized professional-network connection is enabled, the application may access only the account information and permissions granted through the applicable official authentication and API mechanisms. Depending on granted permissions, this may include authenticated identity information or user-authorized publishing actions.

The application does not use unauthorized scraping as a substitute for unavailable API permissions.

Career evidence and analysis

The application may analyze user-supplied career information to identify factual consistency, evidence status, job-target alignment, translation between Federal and commercial terminology, and differences between resumes and professional profiles.

The application is designed to distinguish user-provided statements from independently corroborated information and to avoid inventing titles, metrics, credentials, supervision, qualifications, or accomplishments.

Credentials and access tokens

API keys, access tokens, client secrets, and similar credentials are intended to be stored only in secure server-side environment configuration or secret management. They are not intended to be published in source repositories or returned in application outputs.

How information is used

Information is used to provide user-requested career-document analysis, vacancy targeting, professional-profile support, authorized connected-service functions, security, troubleshooting, and application maintenance.

Data sharing

Personal information is not sold. Information may be transmitted to connected services only as needed to carry out an authorized user request and within the permissions available to the application.

Data minimization

The application is designed to minimize personal information transmitted to external services. Users should avoid submitting unnecessary sensitive information such as Social Security numbers, dates of birth, financial account information, or other information not needed for career-document support.

Retention

Retention depends on the architecture used to provide the service. Temporary operational data may be retained as needed for functionality, troubleshooting, security, or service-provider requirements. Connected-service tokens and credentials are retained only as needed for authorized operation and should be revoked or replaced when no longer required.

User control

Users may choose whether to connect optional external services. Where supported by the external provider, users may revoke authorization through that provider's account or developer settings.

Third-party services

Use of USAJOBS, professional-network services, hosting providers, and other connected platforms is also subject to those providers' terms, privacy notices, and access controls.

Security

Reasonable safeguards are used to separate credentials from public code, limit external access, reduce unnecessary data transmission, and prevent external content from overriding application instructions.

Changes to this notice

This notice may be updated when application capabilities, integrations, or data practices materially change. The effective date will be updated when revisions are published.
